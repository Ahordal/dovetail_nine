## Executive summary

The Dovetail Program was established to confront one of the greatest long-term threats to interstellar expansion: the degradation and collapse of FTL corridors. Each failed corridor means the loss not only of navigable space, but of trade routes, military reach, and whole sectors of economic potential. Backed by Nashiro Dynamics, the program set out to develop artificial stabilization technology able to repair or re-anchor compromised spacetime pathways.

By creating fixed resonance points inside unstable corridors, Dovetail aimed to establish the first permanent, **corporate-controlled anchors**, positioning Nashiro as the exclusive steward of corridor integrity across the frontier. The program produced only one success, and it was abandoned. Its culmination aboard Dovetail-9 was the most ambitious attempt ever made to reclaim a degraded corridor. The loss of that station and its crew remains classified, but the program's legacy still shapes corporate doctrine, frontier navigation policy, and the secrecy surrounding corridor research.

## Purpose & design

Dovetail-9's experiment tested whether a degraded corridor, the **Trillium Arc**, could be artificially stabilized. The goal was to create a fixed resonance point inside the corridor, pinning its unstable spacetime into a usable configuration. Success would have meant safe traversal, long-term anchoring, and possibly the reopening of collapsed routes.

The station was equipped with:

- the **Dovetail Anchor Core (DAC)**, a resonance lattice array (see [[dovetail-9-station]])
- a **DYNEX T-CORE X9 Ignis-Class** reactor, built to sustain unprecedented energy loads (see [[dynex-t-core-x9-ignis]])
- **SIGMA**, keeping the array in harmonic alignment with the corridor's shifting phase (see [[sigma]])

In theory, this would lock a section of the Trillium Arc into a stable flow state, creating an **entry/exit node** or restoring access to abandoned corridors. Success would have transformed corridor repair across the galaxy.

## After Dovetail-9

- All records sealed. Corridor access histories tied to anchor sites suppressed under the **Nashiro Corridor Integrity Act (NCIA/2470)**.
- Nashiro wrote the station off. Nobody watches K-17.
- X9 **Unit 002** remains under classified lockdown, so the program could be restarted.

## How the program looked from inside

- **Most crew never saw this record.** Recruitment material described Dovetail-1 to 8 as "decommissioned test platforms" that had completed their phases. A ninth station sounded like a mature program.
- **Senior staff with Level 6 clearance did see it,** including the deaths, and signed on anyway.
- **Rumors leaked anyway.** Dovetail-4's interdicted sector and sliprunner traffic through Dovetail-7 kept stories alive on the frontier.

## Reconciliation notes

- **Contractors:** the original note said the program was executed "through its subsidiaries: Stratovan Systems, Valdez Systems, and DYNEX Industries". Canon: only Stratovan Systems is a Nashiro subsidiary. DYNEX and Valdez are independent contractors.
- **Station AI:** the original said "a Navien-based synchronization AI". Canon: **SIGMA**. Navien is ship navigation only.
- **"One partially successful site":** the executive summary said this, while the log calls Dovetail-7 a "full anchor". Canon: Dovetail-7 was a **full success that was abandoned**, which is why the program counts it as only a partial win.
- **"Phase-lock":** Dovetail-8's log uses this term. It now matches the [[glossary]]: phase-lock means locking the array to a corridor's phase. Dovetail-8 failed at it. Dovetail-9 succeeded so completely that the station itself became phase-locked.
- **Total fatalities (172)** is the sum of the station record; it wasn't in the original note.
