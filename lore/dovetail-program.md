## Executive summary

The Dovetail Program was established to confront one of the greatest long-term threats to interstellar expansion: the degradation and collapse of FTL corridors. Each failed corridor means the loss not only of navigable space, but of trade routes, military reach, and whole sectors of economic potential. Backed by Nashiro Dynamics, the program set out to develop artificial stabilization technology able to repair or re-anchor compromised spacetime pathways.

By creating fixed resonance points inside unstable corridors, Dovetail aimed to establish the first permanent, **corporate-controlled anchors**, positioning Nashiro as the exclusive steward of corridor integrity across the frontier. The program produced only one success, and it was abandoned. Its culmination aboard Dovetail-9 was the most ambitious attempt ever made to reclaim a degraded corridor. The loss of that station and its crew remains classified, but the program's legacy still shapes corporate doctrine, frontier navigation policy, and the secrecy surrounding corridor research.

## Purpose & design

Dovetail-9's experiment tested whether a degraded corridor, the **Trillium Arc**, could be artificially stabilized. The goal was to create a fixed resonance point inside the corridor, pinning its unstable spacetime into a usable configuration. Success would have meant safe traversal, long-term anchoring, and possibly the reopening of collapsed routes.

The station was equipped with:

- the **Dovetail Anchor Core (DAC)**, a resonance lattice array (see [[dovetail-9-station]])
- a **DYNEX T-CORE X9 Ignis-Class** reactor, built to sustain unprecedented energy loads (see [[dynex-t-core-x9-ignis]])
- **SIGMA**, keeping the array in harmonic alignment with the corridor's shifting phase (see [[sigma]])

In theory, this would lock a section of the Trillium Arc into a stable flow state, creating an **entry/exit node** or restoring access to abandoned corridors. Success would have transformed corridor repair across the galaxy.

## After Dovetail-9

- All records sealed. Corridor access histories tied to anchor sites suppressed under the **Nashiro Corridor Integrity Act (NCIA/2470)**.
- Nashiro wrote the station off. Nobody watches K-17.
- X9 **Unit 002** remains under classified lockdown, so the program could be restarted.

## How the program looked from inside

- **Most crew never saw this record.** Recruitment material described Dovetail-1 to 8 as "decommissioned test platforms" that had completed their phases. A ninth station sounded like a mature program.
- **Senior staff with Level 6 clearance did see it,** including the deaths, and signed on anyway.
- **Rumors leaked anyway.** Dovetail-4's interdicted sector and sliprunner traffic through Dovetail-7 kept stories alive on the frontier.

## Reconciliation notes

- **Contractors:** the original note said the program was executed "through its subsidiaries: Stratovan Systems, Valdez Systems, and DYNEX Industries". Canon: only Stratovan Systems is a Nashiro subsidiary. DYNEX and Valdez are independent contractors.
- **Station AI:** the original said "a Navien-based synchronization AI". Canon: **SIGMA**. Navien is ship navigation only.
- **"One partially successful site":** the executive summary said this, while the log calls Dovetail-7 a "full anchor". Canon: Dovetail-7 was a **full success that was abandoned**, which is why the program counts it as only a partial win.
- **"Phase-lock":** Dovetail-8's log uses this term. It now matches the [[glossary]]: phase-lock means locking the array to a corridor's phase. Dovetail-8 failed at it. Dovetail-9 succeeded so completely that the station itself became phase-locked.
- **Total fatalities (172)** is the sum of the station record; it wasn't in the original note.

## Anchors need constant power **[settled]**

- A pin only holds while it's powered. If an anchor stops, its corridor drifts again. This is why the X9 has no shutdown option.

## What powered the earlier stations **[proposed]**

- **Dovetail-1 to 8 ran on banks of DYNEX fusion cores:** large station versions of the T-Core line (the Mistral's T12 is a 500 MW fusion core).
- **Enough for minor corridors.** Pinning a small, mildly degraded corridor like the Halo Verge takes gigawatts. A fusion bank supplies that, burning on the order of 90 kg of fuel a year per gigawatt, with its waste heat shed by large radiators.
- **Not enough for the Trillium Arc.** A major, deeply degraded corridor takes exawatts, about a billion times more. Fusion can't reach that: at 3.5 EW it would burn around 10 tonnes of fuel a second, and its waste heat would vaporize it.
- **Dovetail-8 proved it.** Its DAC collapse during phase-lock, at the edge of the Arc, showed fusion couldn't hold a corridor that size. That failure is why Nashiro commissioned the X9 for Dovetail-9 (see [[dynex-t-core-x9-ignis]]).
- **Fusion cores can be switched off; the X9 can't.** Dovetail-7's cores could stop at any time, but stopping them drops the pin. The program's escalation runs from proven, limited technology to something no one can switch off.

## Why Dovetail-7 was abandoned **[settled]**

- **It was a test site, not the prize.** The Halo Verge corridor was a minor, low-value route, chosen because it was degraded but safe to experiment on. The anchor's real value was the data: proof the method worked and how to repeat it.
- **The jurisdictional dispute was an excuse.** A local authority claimed the region. Nashiro could have crushed the claim, but fighting it meant drawing attention to a secret program over a corridor it no longer needed. It chose to withdraw and cited jurisdiction in the official record.
- **A skeleton crew was left to run it,** and **sliprunners (pirates)** attacked it again and again. Worthless to Nashiro but valuable to pirates: constant raids explain why Nashiro abandoned it, and its low value explains why it could afford to.
- **Dovetail stations have no external defenses. [settled]** Station security carries small arms, enough for internal incidents but not to turn back sustained pirate attacks.
- **Nashiro's support faded over time.** Early raids brought reinforcements; as the site's value dropped, the reinforcements came less often, then stopped.
- **People died** in the attacks and in the final escape. **[settled]**
- **[proposed]** The station record still lists **0** fatalities for Dovetail-7, because the program only counted deaths during the experiment itself. The skeleton crew's deaths were logged elsewhere as security incidents. A reader who finds both can catch the discrepancy.
- **Its power couldn't safely be shut down,** so it was left running. Its fusion cores could be stopped, but that would drop the pin (see below).
- **[proposed] Why it wasn't scuttled:** destroying a working anchor releases its pin all at once. Dovetail-4 showed what that does: "destabilization spread to the local sector", still interdicted. Scuttling Dovetail-7 risked a disaster in territory another authority claims, and an investigation. Leaving it running was the only quiet option.
- **Now a pirate station run by organized crime.** **[settled]** They keep the anchor alive, patching and refueling it, because a working anchor is a fortune: a private route through the Halo Verge. When it finally fails, the corridor will drift again.
- Document pair: the official record ("abandoned following a jurisdictional dispute") against an internal Level 6 memo (low value, data harvested, not worth contesting).

## Why the Trillium Arc **[settled]**

- **It used to be a major route:** the main corridor between the Perseus Arm's Mid-Rim and the outer Perseus frontier, including the Thrennal Expanse. When it degraded, the outer frontier was cut off. Trade slowed, colonies withered, and only drives like Ravel's could still get through.
- **Whoever anchors it controls the frontier:** the only reliable way in or out of a whole region. The cage from the pitch, made literal.
- **Nobody can claim it.** The lesson of Dovetail-7: the deep Arc is lawless and empty, with no authority to argue jurisdiction.
- **The hardest test.** Dovetail-8 failed at the edge of the Arc; Dovetail-9 went deep with the X9 and SIGMA.
- It explains why the Arc is full of wrecks for Quell to salvage, why Ravel drives matter there, and why X9 Unit 002 is still a threat.
