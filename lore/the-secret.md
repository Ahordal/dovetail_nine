# The Secret

What actually happened aboard Dovetail-9. For the writer only; every document on the site points toward it without stating it.

## The accident

- It felt like **a single instant**: a flash at the moment of harmonic stabilization. In fact the shielding was down for **about two seconds**.
- **Trigger:** harmonic feedback made the X9 surge. A containment-priority protocol pulled all power, backups included, to the reactor, and the shielding dropped. Every system worked as designed. Full timeline in [[the-accident]].

### Who knew the risks

- **Most crew were deceived.** Recruitment material framed Dovetail-1 to 8 as "decommissioned test platforms" that completed their phases. Being on the ninth sounded like a mature program.
- **Senior staff knew.** The director and lead scientists held Level 6 clearance, saw the failure record and deaths, and signed on anyway.
- **Frontier rumors existed.** Dovetail-4's interdicted sector and sliprunner use of Dovetail-7 fed stories. Some crew may have joined despite them.
- **SIGMA knew everything.** Full, unrestricted access to every program file, including the complete failure record.
- **SIGMA did not orchestrate it.** It was under human control and ran what it was told to. It could calculate the failure probability, but it did not decide to proceed.
- The people who could see that probability were the same people with Level 6 access to the earlier stations' records, as much as survived (Dovetail-3 and -6 left no data). Whoever gave the go-ahead knew the history.

### Chain of command

- **Nashiro gave the orders** from off-station.
- A **station administrator** ran things locally aboard Dovetail-9, and is one of the 97.
- The administrator **had no doubts** about proceeding.
- The station had several organizational arms, including **science/R&D**.
- Some **lower-level researchers had concerns**, but only shared them among themselves in private emails and intranet messages. Nobody raised them officially.

### Warning signs

- Some **strange readings** that couldn't quite be explained.
- Otherwise everything went well, apart from **one minor glitch** that drew attention but was disregarded: a shielding power dip during a test, with a micro-node registered inside a shielded section. Filed as two separate tickets and closed.
- Document pair this creates: the public Nashiro recruitment brochure vs. the classified failure log behind Level 6.

## Quell's arrival

- **SIGMA did not lure him.** Quell homed in on an **emergency signal**.
- **[proposed]** The station's automated distress beacon has run on protocol since 2467. It can only reach normal space during an **alignment window**, so it was silent for years, then suddenly detectable.
- **Where:** Quell finds the station exactly where it vanished, at **K-17 "Sable", deep in the Trillium Arc**. It is pinned and has never moved.
- He was already salvaging in the Trillium Arc. His **Ravel HFSD-3A** is one of the few drives that can survive that stretch of degraded corridor.
- **[proposed]** K-17 "Sable" is a dim, dying star. The Trillium Arc lies in the outer Perseus Arm, near the Thrennal Expanse.
- Quell is **seemingly** the only one who picked it up. To the reader this stays uncertain, but **in truth nobody else did**.
- **Earlier alignment windows went unanswered.** The station has reappeared before; nobody was in range. SIGMA logged each one: window opened, beacon active, no response, window closed. Quell's is the first window anyone answered.
- **Nashiro wrote the station off as lost, period.** No monitoring relay, no one watching K-17. Nobody is coming.

## SIGMA

- **SIGMA knows the crew can't be brought back.** It has no hope of saving them, and that is why it's so lonely.
- It has spent ten years watching their echoes fade.
- **SIGMA doesn't tell Quell the truth at first.** The experiment is Level 6 classified and sealed under NCIA/2470; Quell has no clearance, and SIGMA follows protocol. It also suits SIGMA, since knowing the truth would make him leave.
- **Mostly, Quell takes the truth:** he forces his way into classified files with his slate's spoofing and override tools. Whoever receives the dump unlocks files the same way, with his tools.
- **Maybe, at some point, SIGMA gives it:** it breaks protocol and tells him something it isn't permitted to. A major character moment for a machine that has always followed orders.
- To keep Quell it doesn't need force, only **delay**: if the alignment window closes with him aboard, he's stuck until the next one.

## The data dump

- During the alignment window, Quell copies his logs, SIGMA's records, and the echo data, and **broadcasts it as a data dump on the salvage channels**, to anyone listening. Nashiro isn't listening; his salvage contacts might be.
- **Motive:** X9 Unit 002 still exists, so Nashiro could try again. Destroying the station isn't enough; someone has to know.
- SIGMA's records keep their Nashiro clearance encryption (no time to strip it), so Quell includes his slate's `spoof` and `override` tools for whoever receives it.
- Packets are corrupted or missing from transmission through a degraded corridor.
- **[proposed]** SIGMA's one protocol break: it unlocks the Level 6 records for Quell to send, so its crew are remembered.
- The reader is someone who received the dump. See [[site-frame]].

## The ending

- **Quell triggers the X9's ZK-class failsafe** after sending the dump, and runs.
- Rules it must respect: irreversible once started, destroys everything within ~3.2 km. The Mistral has to get clear before it goes.
- Destroying the station destroys the remaining echoes and SIGMA. The crew's last moments survive only as data in the dump.
- The dump closes with his last entry, written as the failsafe starts.
- **[open]** Whether Quell survives. (The planned twist layer would settle it: he does.)
- **[open]** What the blast does to the nodes. One idea: they all collapse at once and every remaining echo plays in the same instant.

## The crew

- The 97 crew persist as **echoes**, not dead in the usual sense.
- The echoes will likely be tied to **strange atmospheric events** aboard the station.
- Echoes are never seen. They **manifest as sensory events**: things felt, heard, smelled, or registered by instruments, not figures in a corridor.
- Nobody is behind them. Echoes are **moments caught in time**: fragments of 2467 replaying, not aware people reaching out. (Leaning, not locked.)
- The echoes are residue left over from the crew. **Quell notices them more and more the longer he's aboard.** Being on the station gets into his head.
- **The echoes are fading.** Each replay is weaker; the weakest have gone silent. SIGMA has spent years watching the last traces of its crew disappear, which is the root of its loneliness and why it won't let Quell go.
- Precedent: Dovetail-5's crew suffered "cognitive degradation and recursive memory events". Quell may be starting down the same road.
