# DYNEX T12 Core

**Primary power reactor of the Mistral.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Manufacturer** | DYNEX Heavy Systems, a division of [[dynex-industries]] |
| **Model** | T12 |
| **Class** | Mid-mass, dual-feed modular power core |
| **Application** | Power for small and medium independent vessels needing variable load balancing (salvage operations, FTL sync systems) |

## Design summary

A rugged, self-contained fusion reactor with integrated plasma capacitors and redundant magnetic containment. Known for reliability over elegance, it is popular among retrofitted freighters, modular salvage rigs, and field-modified ships like the Mistral.

Quell chose the T12 because it tolerates unstable power draws, essential for the fluctuating demand of his retrieval arm, his volatile FTL drive, and isolated environmental systems.

## Specifications

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dimensions** | 2.4 m long × 1.6 m wide × 1.8 m high |
| **Mass** | ~5.8 metric tons |
| **Output** | Up to 900 MW burst, 500 MW sustained |
| **Redundancy** | Triple-layer magnetic bottling with localized fuse dampeners |
| **Stabilizers** | Dual vented flux coils with auto-trim override (manual only on the Mistral) |
| **Cooling** | Closed loop, routed through the auxiliary spine and vented via side-panel exchangers |

## Operational quirks (Quell's T12)

- **Manual trim.** Quell removed the auto-trim routines after repeated misfires during FTL transitions. He tunes plasma flow by hand at an exposed valve cluster in the aft engineering bay.
- **Vibration drift.** Partial weld faults in the mounting cradle make the reactor shudder at peak load. He embedded shock-damp gel panels to compensate.
- **Warnings disabled.** Internal alerts are rerouted to a single red bulb above the diagnostics bench, labeled "IF THIS IS ON, YOU'RE TOO LATE."

## Installation

- **Location:** rear engineering compartment, port side
- **Mount:** six-point reinforced bracing frame with exterior service hatch
- **Shielding:** basic industrial radiation lining, plus scavenged high-frequency dampening mesh installed by Quell

## Field notes

- Quell maintains it by hand every cycle, with a toolkit of non-standard parts he assembled himself.
- Not integrated into the ship's AI, by choice. Diagnostics run through a patched-in analog readout.
- A past capacitor overload scorched part of the interior paneling. He never repainted it; he says the burn pattern is "good luck".

## Canon notes

- The T12 is the DYNEX Heavy Systems division's analog, no-AI ethos in practice. It's why Quell trusts the DYNEX name, and part of why the X9's harmonic flaw is a betrayal.
- The "high-frequency dampening mesh" shows that harmonic dampening tech is common enough to salvage.
