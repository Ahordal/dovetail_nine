# Dovetail-9 Station

## Scale

- **Crew:** 166, in seven divisions. See [[crew]].
- **Main hull [settled]:** about **400 m** long and **90 to 100 m** tall. **[proposed]** About **100 m wide** at its fullest, with a height of about **96 m** (12 decks of 8 m): a roughly square cross-section, a chunky block as in the exterior image. About 3.8 million m³. Pressurized; all living and working space. Sized up from 180 m so the station needs its lift systems; much of the volume is machinery (Core Chamber, shielding, R&D), not crew space.
- **Decks:** **12**, about 5 m floor to ceiling, with service spaces between. Implies artificial gravity (mechanism **[open]**).
- **[proposed] Two comms spires** stand on Deck 01, as in the exterior image: a taller one left of centre and a shorter twin-tipped one to its right, each a tapering lattice mast on a stepped base, with smaller masts, dishes and equipment boxes clustered around them. Red beacons on the tips.
- **[proposed] Decks are numbered from the top down.** Deck 01 sits under the antennas, the natural place for command and communications; Deck 12 is the lowest. Each deck takes about 8 m of the hull's height, floor plus service space.
- **Decks are shared [settled].** No deck is given over to one thing. Large features (the docking bay, the tram, the Core Chamber, engineering) take up a section of the decks they sit on, and the rest of each deck holds other rooms and corridors. A deck is a level, not a department.
- **Core Chamber [settled]:** the X9's trap housing is compact (see [[dynex-t-core-x9-ignis]]), but its chamber is vast: a spherical containment dome about **three decks** tall, three concentric shells (coolant and coils, radiation shielding, structural sphere) around a billion-tonne micro black hole held at the exact center. The outer shell ties into the station's keel to carry the singularity's weight; its gravity is strong enough that loose objects in the chamber drift toward it. No physical access during runtime, so it is sealed and seen through polarized lead glass from an **observation gallery**, which has its own restricted engineering terminal.
- **[proposed] Where the Core Chamber sits:** at the middle of the hull, 200 m along and centred across its width, spanning **Decks 05 to 07** with the black hole at the middle of Deck 06. The black hole outweighs the station, so the station balances around it, and the array is symmetrical either side of it.
- **[proposed] Deck 06 is the spine.** The **keel** is a structural beam running the hull's full length at Deck 06 (a keel needn't be at the bottom): both arrays bolt to its ends and the chamber ties into it. The **conduits** run out from the chamber along it to both ends, so the X9's power moves sideways along the hull. The **tram** runs beside them, passing the chamber alongside it, not through it.
- **[proposed] The tram passes engineering in a shielded tube** along the side of Deck 06, sealed off from the reactor spaces, with no stop between the two lift cores. The tube was built to be the safe way past the chamber; with the shielding down it became a run of hot spots.
- **[proposed] The tram line is the most dangerous route aboard.** The radiation hot spots are worst near the chamber and along the conduit runs ([[phase-mechanics]]), and the tram follows both.
- **[proposed] Engineering** is the section around the Core Chamber, about **137 to 263 m along on Decks 05 to 07**, running out to meet both lift cores: reactor support, the Hawking Harvest Rings' heat systems, the coolant loop, and the observation gallery. Directly above the docking bay. **Two vertical access shafts** run through it, one either side of the chamber, roughly midway between the chamber and the section's edge (about 164 and 236 m).
- **[proposed] Experiment control** is a shared Science and Engineering room on **Deck 05**, just inside engineering's edge, with **R&D** (Science's lab, on Deck 5 per [[tobias-quell]]) next door outside engineering. It has **two doors**: one from R&D (the sealed lock in Quell's log) and one from engineering. A **hatch** on its far side opens into the left vertical access shaft: a way in that skips the sealed lock.
  - Science designed and called the run; Engineering ran the X9, the array and the shielding from here; SIGMA executed. The DYNEX, Valdez and Stratovan contractors had their own stations ([[crew]]).
  - **Everyone was in the room** at the moment of the accident, including the administrator who signed the bank B waiver. Its records, and the bodies still at their stations, show who sat where and who gave the go-ahead ([[the-secret]]).
  - Direct runs to the X9 and the array conduits on the Deck 06 spine below.
- **[proposed] SIGMA's core** is a sealed **column about 5 m across, centred at 200 m on the Core Chamber's axis, rising from Deck 04 up into command on Deck 01** (see [[sigma]]). Its small **operations room** sits at the base on Deck 04, where Engineering services the hardware.
  - **One vertical line through the station:** the black hole on Deck 06, SIGMA above it, the comms spires above that. The reactor and the mind stacked on one axis.
  - **Command is built around it.** The column's top stands in the middle of command on Deck 01, so the station's senior staff worked around SIGMA. On Decks 02 and 03 it's a sealed column people walk around: SIGMA is present on every deck it passes.
  - A tall stack suits the hardware: one coolant riser down its length to the spine loop, short runs to the X9 below and the spires above.
  - **No team of its own.** Command & Administration's operations staff work with SIGMA day to day; Engineering keeps up the hardware ([[crew]]).
  - Short runs one deck down to experiment control and the X9, and up to command and the comms spires on Deck 01. Its heat goes into the coolant loop along the spine to the radiators.
  - Hard to reach: engineering and the chamber's hot spots lie below it. The hardware is radiation-hardened; Quell isn't. When he reaches it, he stands over the black hole SIGMA has watched for ten years.
- **[proposed] Harmonic shielding** comes in two parts (see [[the-accident]]):
  - **Emitters**, spread through the hull rather than in one room: a shield layer around the crewed sections, heaviest along the Deck 06 conduit runs and where the conduits enter the hull at each end, since harmonics come in along them.
  - **The shielding plant**, on **Deck 07 inside engineering**, directly under the spine and the chamber: the switch room where the primary bus, backup bus and capacitor banks meet, and **two capacitor halls, one either side of the chamber**, Bank A and Bank B. Short runs to the Harvest Rings beside it and the Deck 06 emitters above it.
  - **How the banks connect:** each bank charges from the chamber's Harvest Rings by way of the primary bus, and feeds up into the emitters along the Deck 06 conduits. In normal running the bus powers the emitters directly and the banks sit charged, bridging the gap if the bus trips. **[proposed]** The backup bus also draws from the Harvest Rings, by a separate route, so one surge at the source can reach both buses. It was only as separate as its designers assumed, which fits "every system worked as designed" ([[the-accident]]).
  - **The halls need crew access** for inspection, testing and module replacement; someone read bank B below spec before the run. Each hall runs out to its vertical access shaft, with a door into it.
  - **Valdez's own section,** with its own door and clearance inside Nashiro's engineering ([[crew]], contractor teams).
  - **The shield's edge runs along the docking bay's ceiling.** The bay is outside it, which is why a docked ship sits unshielded in the field ([[the-secret]]).
  - Next to the chamber and the conduits, so among the worst radiation hot spots ([[phase-mechanics]]): reaching the room where the accident began costs Quell.
- **Radiators:** the station sheds its waste heat (the X9's ~360 MW Hawking glow plus ordinary systems) through radiator panels along the Anchor Core array arms. The amber lights along the trusses in the exterior image can be read as their markers.
- **Ways of moving through the station [settled]:**
  - **Lifts:** vertical, between the 12 decks.
  - **Tram:** the horizontal lift, running the length of the hull on **one main deck only, Deck 06**. Crossing the hull end to end means getting to that deck first. At 400 m, walking end to end takes five minutes or more, longer hauling gear or suited.
  - **Access shafts:** maintenance shafts, both vertical and horizontal, for crew and repairs. **[proposed]** The way around when a lift or the tram is down.
  - **Corridors** on every deck, on foot.
  - **[proposed] Two lift cores, at about 135 m and 265 m along,** each serving all 12 decks. They are the only positions that reach every deck: inside Deck 01 (130 to 270 m), flush against engineering's ends, clear of the docking bay, and inside Deck 12 (100 to 300 m). The centre of the hull can't take a lift, since engineering and the bay fill it from Deck 05 down. The shafts are solid bulkheads, stopping at every deck. Which side each lift opens on is left to the deck plans; on Decks 05 to 07 the inner side faces engineering.
  - **[proposed] Lift lobbies on Deck 06,** on the outer side of each lift, are the tram's two main stops: where people change between lift and tram, and a natural place for public terminals.
  - The routes of the access shafts beyond engineering's two **[open]**.
- **Anchor Core array:** the Dovetail Anchor Core's resonance lattice. Open, unpressurized truss structures extending **straight out from both ends of the hull** along the same axis, for an overall length of about **1.7 km** (keeping the exterior image's proportions, where the hull is about a quarter of the whole). Accessed only by suited maintenance crews.
- **Shape:** long, linear, accelerator-like. Bracket-shaped resonance coils and emitter housings line the trusses at even intervals, like the stages of a particle accelerator, with conduits running back into the hull. A heavy industrial frame, not a ring or gate.
- **[proposed] Reinforcement rings:** the bracket-shaped coil housings above, seen as octagonal frames that wrap the truss at intervals, as in the exterior image, with at least one close to the hull at each end. They carry amber marker lights (the radiator markers, see Radiators above) that blink faster than the spires' red beacons, and no antennas.
- **How it works:** the structure lies **along** the corridor. The emitters channel resonance down its length and focus it at a point **just beyond the forward end**, where the primary node was meant to form: in the corridor, beside the station, not inside it.
- **The aft end is a counter-resonator,** balancing the forward focus so the structure doesn't tear itself apart. Both tips glow; only the forward one forms a node.
- **Why the array is big:** like a radio antenna, it must be sized to what it resonates with. To engage a corridor it has to be built at the corridor's scale.
- **Fits the program's stated aim:** the Experiment note says a stable anchor could create "an entry/exit node" in the corridor.
- Visual reference: `reference/dovetail-9-exterior.png`. Side view of the hull, drawn from this file: `reference/hull-side-view/`.
- **Conduits** carry power and data from the array into the hull, to the X9 and SIGMA. During the accident, with the shielding down, the harmonics entered along them (see [[the-accident]]).
- **Scale references:** 166 crew in a 400 m hull is sparse, closer to an automated plant than a submarine: large stretches are machinery SIGMA runs alone, which suits a station dead for ten years. The Mistral (58 m) is about a seventh of the hull's length.
- The X9 failsafe's ~3.2 km radius is about twice the structure's overall length.

## Docking bay **[proposed]**

- **Where:** low in the hull, about **Decks 08 to 11**, and **centred along the hull, under the superstructure**. In the exterior image the lit opening starts a little more than halfway down the hull and ends near the bottom, about 30 to 35 m tall. (Heights can be read off the image; lengths can't, since it sees the hull at an angle.) The Mistral (58 × 16.5 × 11.2 m) flies in with room to spare.
- **Unpressurized and open to space.** The image shows no door, only light spilling out.
- **In cross-section it's one large open rectangle** spanning those four decks, with corridors and rooms on Decks 08 to 11 wrapped around it, and Decks 07 and 12 above and below. The biggest open space in the hull that anyone can see into.
- **Ships dock nose first,** tail facing out of the opening. So the bay must reach at least ~65 m into the hull for the Mistral; at about 70 m deep in a ~100 m wide hull, that leaves ~30 m behind it for corridors and rooms.
- **Under the Core Chamber.** The bay's ceiling (top of Deck 08) meets the bottom of the chamber; a docked ship sits about 25 m below the black hole.
- **Docking ring:** a ring of four clamps inside the bay that closes around a docked ship's hull. **[proposed]** "Docking ring" is the system's name, not its shape: there is no ring. **Four independent clamp arms**, numbered 1 to 4, hold the ship: two reach down from the bay ceiling and two up from the floor, each jointed, ending in a pad that locks onto the hull. The ship's sides stay clear for the umbilical. "Clamp 2 jammed" is one specific arm. A docked ship is held clear of the floor (the Mistral has no landing gear), which also lines its hatch up with Airlock B. The clamps in Quell's log ("DOCKING RING. Clamp 2 jammed…", [[tobias-quell]]) are these.
- **Boarding umbilical:** a short pressurized tube from **Airlock B**, on **Deck 09** in the bay wall, to the docked ship's hatch. How Quell crosses without a suit.
- **[proposed] Both airlocks have an umbilical.** A's is pulled back against its wall, with no ship on that side.
- **[proposed] Airlock A** faces Airlock B from the bay's opposite end wall, level with it on **Deck 09**. In the side view A is on the left of the bay and B on the right. Both airlocks are small rooms, about 6.5 m, set into the bay walls.
- **[proposed] Both airlocks hold their own suits.** Each has two parts, like the ISS's Quest airlock: an equipment lock where suits are stored and put on, and a smaller crew lock that is pumped down. Airlock B opens straight onto vacuum whenever no ship is docked, and crews go out through either airlock to work the bay (the jammed clamp in Quell's log). Quell's Ravel Protek-3A would be stowed in Airlock B's equipment lock.
- **[proposed] Airlock machinery** fills Deck 08 above each airlock and its ready room, sealed with no crew access: **air recovery tanks** that store the air pumped out of the airlock instead of venting it, a **depressurisation pump**, a **valve manifold** whose riser drops through the floor into the airlock, the cycle's **controls and power**, and the **ram** that drives the outer door in the bay wall.
- **[proposed] Airlocks and ready rooms are low rooms:** about **4.5 m** to the ceiling, with **3 m** doors. A smaller airlock pumps out less air each cycle, and cargo goes through the cargo airlock below A, so the airlocks only need to fit suited crew. The rest of Deck 09 above them is service space joined to the machinery on Deck 08: the station's air return duct up into the tanks, a supply pipe pair from the station side, the manifold riser down to a vent in the airlock ceiling, and the ram's rod down to the outer door, which lifts into this space when open.
- **[proposed] Ready rooms,** one for each airlock on Deck 09, running from the lift to the airlock: the level, walk-in route to both airlocks. Each holds the airlock's pressure door and door panel, a bench and an emergency suit locker; the main suits stay in the airlocks' equipment locks.
- **[proposed] Airlock door panels,** two for each airlock: one in the ready room beside the pressure door, one inside the airlock. Both run the **depressurisation cycle** and the **door locks**.
  - **Each ready room panel also runs its airlock's umbilical,** so it can be extended or pulled back from the pressurized side, without anyone in the airlock.
- **[proposed] Cargo hold,** its floor a deck above the bay floor, level with the raised cargo dock. The **cargo door**, on Deck 10 directly below Airlock A, opens into the **cargo airlock**, then the **cargo ready room**, then a **one-deck hallway** that runs back past the left lift, then opens into a **two-storey storage area** (Decks 09 and 10) reaching to about 105 m. The lift stops in the hallway, its one cargo stop. Habitation services' stores and logistics ([[crew]]); supplies come in by ship and go up by that lift.
  - **Shelving** at the back of the storage area: three levels carried on a truss frame, posts tied by diagonal bracing, each level a shallow truss with a deck on top. Mostly empty, a few boxes here and there; more left loose in the cargo airlock.
  - **A hold crane** on a ceiling rail the length of the storage area, its trolley and grab working the open floor in front of the shelving.
  - **[proposed] Cargo airlock:** a full-deck-height room about **8 m** long, between the cargo door and the hallway, so the hold stays pressurized while loads come in from the cargo dock. Both its doors are a full deck high, about 7.5 m, for the biggest loads. The inner one opens into the cargo ready room, with a door panel on each side (cycle and door locks).
  - **Its machinery** fills Deck 11 below the cargo airlock and cargo ready room, from the lift to the bay wall, the same 14 m as the machinery above Airlock A. Sealed, no crew access: bigger air recovery tanks than the crew plant's, since the cargo airlock holds far more air, two pumps, a valve manifold with a riser up to a vent in the airlock floor, the controls, and the ram for the cargo door, which drops into this space when open.
  - **One plant on two levels.** A pipe pair runs down inside the wall beside the lift, through Decks 09 and 10, joining the Deck 11 machinery to the machinery above Airlock A on Deck 08, so either airlock can draw on the other's tanks and pumps.
  - **[proposed] Cargo ready room:** the end of the hallway, about **6 m** from the lift to the cargo airlock, walled off on the storage side. Suit lockers, a bench and the cargo airlock's inner door panel. Crews suit up here to work the cargo dock, moving loads the gantry sets down there into the airlock. Cargo passes through it on the way to storage.
- **[proposed] Gantry crane:** a rail the length of the bay ceiling with a trolley and grab, sharing the ceiling with clamps 1 and 2. It lifts cargo out of a docked ship's hatch and carries it across the bay. Small items can go by hand through the umbilical instead; the Mistral's DMRA-5 arm ([[duradyne-dmra-5]]) can hand loads up to it. Ten years on it sits parked on its rail, visible through the airlock windows but out of reach without a suit.
- **[proposed] Cargo dock:** a raised platform outside the cargo door, one deck above the bay floor, its deck a straight continuation of the cargo airlock's floor, about 10 m deep, on legs down to the floor, where the gantry sets loads down before they're moved into the hold. An **open ladder** runs from the dock down to the bay floor, in the gap between the bay wall and the dock's first leg, with grab rails rising above the dock's deck.
- **[proposed] Docking control,** on Deck 10 from the bay wall to the right lift, under Airlock B's ready room: a **near floor-to-ceiling window** onto the bay and a long **control desk** facing it, with a traffic board on the back wall. It runs the **docking clamps, bay lights, gantry crane and docking traffic**. Pressurized, with no door to the bay. The right lift opens into it. From here the whole bay, and any docked ship, is in plain view.
- **[proposed] Bay workshop,** on Deck 11 below docking control, at bay-floor level: a pressurized repair shop for docked ships and the bay's own gear (clamps, gantry, umbilicals), with a desk under a small **window** onto the bay, a workbench and tool board, a rolling tool chest, welding gas bottles, crated spares, a cable reel and a parts cabinet. It has **no door to the bay**, so it needs no airlock of its own; crews go out through Airlock B. The right lift opens into it.
- **[proposed] The bay floor runs on under the cargo machinery** on Deck 11, to the left lift.
- **[proposed] Door states as Quell finds them.** Every door is a game mechanism; the side view colours each one unlocked or locked. First-pass rule: doors onto vacuum are locked, except Airlock B's outer door, in use with the Mistral docked; doors inside the pressurized station are unlocked.
  - **Unlocked:** Airlock B's outer and inner doors, Airlock A's inner door, the cargo airlock's inner door, and the lift doors into R&D, the Deck 06 lobbies, both ready rooms, the cargo hallway, docking control and the bay workshop.
  - **Locked:** Airlock A's outer door and the cargo door.
  - Off this drawing, the R&D lock into experiment control is **sealed** (Quell's log, [[tobias-quell]]).
  - Which doors end up locked, and with what kind of lock (sealed, no power, clearance, as on the mockup's badges), is **[open]**.
- **Fits "visible, unreachable"** ([[mistral]]): cut off from the umbilical, he can still see the Mistral in the bay, through the airlock windows or from docking control, but can't reach it without crossing vacuum. What cuts him off is **[open]**.
- **Means Quell boards low in the station.** Most of what he needs is above him, so the lifts and climbs matter from the first move.
- Reconciles "docking bay" ([[mistral]]) with "docking ring" (Quell's log): the ring is inside the bay.
- **"Corridor" in both senses [settled].** A corridor inside the station is just a corridor. The routes through the Arc are **FTL corridors**; say "FTL corridor" wherever the two could be confused.

## Deck 12 **[proposed]**

- **Under the bay, about 100 to 300 m along,** both lifts reaching it. The station's water lives here, the heaviest thing it keeps, as low as it can go.
- **Water plant** at the left end, beyond the left lift: filter columns, a pump, treatment and controls.
- **Water tank halls** either side of the middle: large reserve tanks on saddles, walked between for inspection.
- **Waste plant** at the right end, beyond the right lift: a digester, a compactor and a pump.
- **One water header** runs the deck, feeding every tank, filter and pump, behind the lift shafts where it crosses them. It stops short of the fuel section.
- Maintenance runs it ([[crew]]); hydroponics on Deck 03 draws its water from here.
- **Backup generators and fuel,** in the middle section under the Mistral, between the two tank halls: two **backup generators** that power the station when the X9 is offline, and **fuel tanks** on saddles. One fuel header feeds the generators and runs two **risers up through the bay floor** to **fuel hose stations** on the bay floor either side of the floor clamps, where docked ships are refuelled. The generators burn the same fuel the bay hands out.
- **No radiation refuge anywhere aboard.** Nobody believed radiation could get inside the station, so none was built; when it did, the crew had nowhere to go ([[the-accident]]). A storm shelter against space weather was also considered and dropped: a 2400s hull shields ordinary space radiation, and K-17 is a dim star.

## Defenses **[settled]**

- **No external defenses.** Like every Dovetail station, it relies on secrecy and remoteness. Station security carries small arms for internal incidents only.
- Nothing stops a ship from approaching and docking, which is how Quell gets aboard.
